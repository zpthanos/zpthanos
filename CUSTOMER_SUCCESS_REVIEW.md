## Three representative customer outcomes

### 1. Recovery and trust

A customer faced a compromised ecommerce site and disrupted operations.

**Action:** Coordinated recovery from verified off-server backups, rotated credentials and validated the complete customer journey before relaunch.

**Result:** The store returned safely with working catalogue, cart and checkout flows, while the customer received clear information about risk and next steps.

[Read the recovery story →](https://github.com/zpthanos/production-stories/blob/main/stories/06-malware-recovery.md)

### 2. Onboarding and adoption

A retailer needed to operate a catalogue of more than 2,000 products without routine developer dependence.

**Action:** Configured the initial workflows, created practical guidance and trained the owner on catalogue, order and refund work.

**Result:** The owner could complete routine administration independently.

[Read the onboarding story →](https://github.com/zpthanos/production-stories/blob/main/stories/05-woocommerce-administration-handover.md)

Slow product pages undermined the customer experience.

**Action:** Diagnosed front-end and caching overhead, introduced controlled improvements and re-tested the buying journey.

**Result:** PageSpeed improved from below 20 to above 75 without breaking catalogue, cart or checkout behaviour.

## How I handle support tickets

1. **Clarify the objective and impact.**
2. **Reproduce the issue and collect evidence.**
3. **Narrow down likely causes and dependencies.**
4. **Coordinate the right technical or service owner.**
5. **Keep the customer informed with useful updates.**
6. **Verify the complete customer outcome before closure.**
7. **Document the finding so future support is faster and clearer.**

## Onboarding and enablement approach

A useful onboarding plan should help a customer understand:

- What the product or workflow is meant to achieve
- What must be configured first
- How to access support and escalate risk
- Which early milestone demonstrates value
- What routine work the customer should become able to complete
- How adoption and unresolved friction will be reviewed
- Where documentation and follow-up guidance live

My strongest public onboarding evidence is the 2,000+ product WooCommerce handover, where the goal was not merely configuration. The goal was independent customer operation.

## Risk and customer-health thinking

The customer signals I already use in production work include:

- Repeated or unresolved support issues
- Critical workflows that cannot be verified
- Security or access concerns
- Slow performance affecting customer journeys
- Dependence on one person or undocumented knowledge
- Delayed onboarding tasks
- Confusion about responsibilities or next actions
- Recurring friction that should become documentation or product feedback
