## Scenario

A growing technology company is introducing Ubuntu-based infrastructure and wants a clearer support and adoption process for its technical users.

The customer needs:

- A practical onboarding path
- Clear support and escalation expectations
- Early identification of adoption risk
- Useful customer documentation
- A repeatable way to communicate with several users
- Structured feedback that product and support teams can review

## Customer objectives

1. Understand the product and support model.
2. Complete the initial setup safely.
3. Reach an early, visible success milestone.
4. Know how to request help and what evidence to provide.
5. Reduce repeated friction through documentation.
6. Maintain confidence as usage expands.

## 30-day onboarding plan

### Days 1–5: objectives and readiness

- Confirm stakeholders, environment and intended outcomes.
- Agree the first meaningful success milestone.
- Identify access, security and dependency risks.
- Explain support channels, severity and escalation expectations.

### Days 6–14: guided activation

- Introduce the product workflow in practical language.
- Complete initial configuration with documented checks.
- Record questions, blockers and unclear terminology.
- Verify that the customer can repeat the critical workflow.

### Days 15–21: enablement

- Deliver a short role-based guide for administrators and users.
- Run a practical question-and-answer session.
- Confirm who owns routine actions and who owns escalations.
- Review unresolved friction and adoption risk.

### Days 22–30: value review

- Compare progress with the agreed success milestone.
- Review support activity and repeated questions.
- Capture product feedback and documentation gaps.
- Agree the next adoption objective and engagement plan.

## Support-request workflow

| Field | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| **Customer objective** | What the user is trying to achieve |
| **Business impact** | Who is affected and how urgently |
| **Environment** | Relevant platform, access and configuration context |
| **Reproduction** | Clear steps and expected versus actual behaviour |
| **Evidence** | Logs, screenshots, timestamps or system details |
| **Owner** | Customer Success, Support, Product or another dependency |
| **Customer update** | What is known, what happens next and when to expect progress |
| **Verification** | How the complete customer outcome will be confirmed |
| **Documentation** | What should be recorded to prevent repeated friction |

## Customer-health signals

### Healthy

- Onboarding milestones are completed on time.
- Relevant users attend enablement sessions.
- Support requests contain useful evidence.
- Critical workflows are verified.
- The customer can explain the value achieved so far.

### Watch closely

- Repeated questions indicate unclear documentation.
- One stakeholder holds all operational knowledge.
- Important setup tasks remain incomplete.
- Support cases are repeatedly reopened.
- The customer cannot identify the next value milestone.

### High risk

- A critical workflow remains blocked.
- Security, access or ownership is unclear.
- Expectations differ across the customer and internal teams.
- The customer has stopped engaging during onboarding.
- Business impact is increasing without an agreed action plan.

## Multi-customer adoption communication

### Audience

Newly onboarded technical customers who have completed setup but have not reached the first adoption milestone.

### Digital touchpoints

1. **Welcome message** — success milestone, support route and first action.
2. **Practical guide** — one short workflow with verification steps.
3. **Risk check** — common blockers and a clear route to ask for help.
4. **Value review** — progress question, feedback prompt and next milestone.

### Measures

- Activation or milestone completion
- Guide engagement
- Support requests generated by unresolved blockers
- Repeated questions requiring documentation improvement
- Customers ready for deeper adoption conversations

## Product-feedback brief

### Observation

Customers repeatedly ask where to find the information required for an effective support request.

### Impact

Cases take longer to diagnose, customers repeat context and support teams spend time collecting basic evidence.

### Proposed improvement

Add a role-based support-request checklist to onboarding material and surface it at the point where users open a case.

### Evidence to collect

- Frequency of incomplete requests
- Most commonly missing fields
- Time spent requesting additional context
- Customer feedback on the revised checklist

### Success measure

A larger proportion of new support requests arrive with usable impact, environment and reproduction information.

## Documentation sample

### Before opening a support request

Please include:

1. What you are trying to achieve.
2. Who or what is affected.
3. What you expected to happen.
4. What happened instead.
5. The time the issue occurred.
6. Relevant screenshots, logs or error messages.
7. Any recent changes to access, configuration or infrastructure.

This gives Support enough context to begin useful investigation without making the customer repeat the same story several times, a ritual nobody has ever enjoyed.
